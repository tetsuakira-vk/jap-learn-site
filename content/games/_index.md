## Which game should I play?

**Learning hiragana or katakana for the first time:** Start with [Memory Match](/games/match/). Flipping cards and making connections is one of the most effective ways to get characters to stick — the brain remembers better when it has to *retrieve* information under mild pressure. Do a full hiragana set before moving to katakana.

**Want to drill vocabulary quickly:** [Kanji Quest](/games/battle/) is the one. The battle format creates just enough pressure to make answers feel urgent — and urgency means faster recall. Set it to JLPT N5 mode and work through the vocab list. When you can survive 10+ rounds without taking damage, you know the set.

**Working on speed and reaction time:** [Rhythm](/games/rhythm/) is uniquely good for this. Being able to *recognise* a character is one thing — doing it fast enough to actually use it in conversation is another. The falling notes force you to respond in real time, which trains the kind of instant recall that reading fluency depends on.

## Why games work for language learning

Repetition is what makes vocabulary stick — but mindless repetition doesn't work. Your brain filters out information it decides isn't important, and drilling flashcards in a quiet room often falls into that category.

Games change the equation by adding **stakes**. Even small stakes — health points, a timer, a combo streak — are enough to signal to your brain that this information matters right now. That signal makes the memory stronger.

The games here aren't a replacement for study. They're a way to get the repetitions in without it feeling like work. Spend 10 minutes on Kanji Quest at the end of a study session and you'll remember more the next day than if you'd spent those 10 minutes on flashcards.

## Tips for getting the most from each game

**Kanji Quest** — Don't just survive. When you answer wrong and take damage, make a note of that word or character. Come back to it in the [vocabulary pages](/vocab/) and understand *why* before you play again. Dying to the same monster twice is wasted time.

**Rhythm** — Start on a slow speed setting. The goal isn't to hit every note — it's to recognise every character before it disappears. Once recognition is automatic, increase the speed. Rush the difficulty and you just practise guessing.

**Memory Match** — Resist the urge to click randomly. Before flipping the second card, say the reading of the first one out loud. That small step forces active recall rather than pattern matching, which is what you actually need.
