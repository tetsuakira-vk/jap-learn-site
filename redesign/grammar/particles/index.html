Core particles

  • は — topic
  • が — subject
  • を — object
  • に — target / time
  • で — place / means
  • へ — direction
  • の — possessive

Connector particles

  • と — with / and
  • や — and (non-exhaustive)
  • も — also / too
  • か — or / question

Range & limit

  • から — from / because
  • まで — until / to
  • より — than
  • だけ — only
  • しか — only (+ neg)
  • ほど — about / extent

Sentence-final

  • ね — seeking agreement
  • よ — asserting

Particles are small words that attach to nouns, verbs, and adjectives to show their grammatical role. Unlike English prepositions, Japanese particles come after the word they mark. Click any example sentence to hear it.

Core Particles

基本助詞
は
wa
Topic marker
Marks what the sentence is about
は marks the topic of a sentence — the thing you're making a statement about. The topic is often (but not always) the grammatical subject. It can also signal contrast: "as for X (but not Y)…"
私は学生です。
I am a student.
これは本です。
This is a book.
私は行きますが、彼は行きません。
I'm going, but he isn't. (contrast)
は vs が: は marks the topic; が marks the subject. Use が to introduce new information or answer "who/what?" Use は for known information or to make a general statement about something.
が
ga
Subject marker
Marks the grammatical subject; highlights new information
が marks the grammatical subject. It introduces new or emphasised information, answers "who?" or "what?", and is required after certain adjectives (好き、嫌い、上手、できる) and with potential and want-to forms.
猫が来た。
The cat came. (new information)
日本語が好きです。
I like Japanese.
誰が来ましたか？
Who came?
を
o (written wo)
Direct object marker
Marks what the verb acts upon
を marks the direct object of a transitive verb — the thing that receives the action. It also marks the path through or along which movement occurs.
ご飯を食べます。
I eat rice.
日本語を勉強しています。
I am studying Japanese.
公園を歩きます。
I walk through the park. (path)
に
ni
Target / location / time
Direction of movement, location of existence, point in time, indirect object
に is one of the most versatile particles. It marks: destination of movement (going to); location of existence (where something is/exists); a specific time; and the indirect object (recipient of giving/receiving verbs).
学校に行きます。
I'm going to school. (destination)
机の上に本があります。
There's a book on the desk. (existence)
三時に会いましょう。
Let's meet at 3 o'clock. (time)
友達にプレゼントをあげました。
I gave a present to my friend. (indirect object)
に vs で: に marks where something exists or the destination of movement. で marks where an action takes place. 図書館に本があります (the book exists at the library) vs 図書館で勉強します (I study at the library).
で
de
Location of action / means
Where an action happens; the method or tool used
で marks the location where an action takes place (contrast with に which marks existence). It also marks the means, material, or method used to do something, and the cause of a state.
図書館で勉強します。
I study at the library. (location of action)
バスで学校に行きます。
I go to school by bus. (means)
日本語で話してください。
Please speak in Japanese. (means/language)
へ
e (written he)
Direction
Movement toward a destination (interchangeable with に in many cases)
へ marks direction of movement. In most sentences it's interchangeable with に for destination, but へ emphasises the direction of travel rather than the arrival point. More formal/literary than に.
東京へ行きます。
I'm going to Tokyo.
日本へようこそ！
Welcome to Japan!
の
no
Possessive / nominaliser
Links two nouns; turns verbs and adjectives into noun phrases
の connects two nouns showing possession, belonging, or description — equivalent to English "of" or 's. It also nominalises a verb or adjective phrase, turning it into a noun.
私の本
My book (possession)
日本の文化が好きです。
I like Japanese culture. (of)
走るのが好きです。
I like running. (nominaliser)

Connector Particles

接続助詞
と
to
With / and (exhaustive) / quotation
と connects nouns meaning "and" (exhaustive — lists everything) or "with" (accompanying). It also marks quotations and thoughts after verbs like 言う (say) and 思う (think).
猫と犬が好きです。
I like cats and dogs.
友達と映画を見ました。
I watched a film with a friend.
行きたいと思います。
I think I want to go. (quotation)
や
ya
And (non-exhaustive)
Lists examples — implies there are others
や lists nouns as examples, implying the list is not complete. "A や B" means "A and B (and other things like them)." Use と when you mean to list everything; use や when giving examples.
本や雑誌を読みます。
I read books and magazines (and so on).
東京や大阪に行きたいです。
I want to go to Tokyo, Osaka, and places like that.
も
mo
Also / too / even
も replaces は or が to mean "also," "too," or "even." Used with negatives it means "either" or "neither." Also used to express large/surprising quantities with numbers.
私も行きます。
I'm going too.
彼女も来ませんでした。
She didn't come either.
三時間も待ちました。
I waited for three whole hours!
か
ka
Question marker / or
か at the end of a sentence turns it into a question. Between nouns it means "or." It also embeds questions inside statements (indirect questions).
日本語を勉強していますか？
Are you studying Japanese?
コーヒーか紅茶はいかがですか？
Would you like coffee or tea?
何時に来るか知りません。
I don't know what time they're coming. (indirect)

Range Particles

範囲助詞
から
kara
From / because
から marks a starting point in space or time ("from"), and also marks reason or cause ("because," "since").
東京から大阪まで新幹線で行きます。
I'm going from Tokyo to Osaka by Shinkansen.
九時から始まります。
It starts from 9 o'clock.
眠いから、もう寝ます。
I'm sleepy, so I'm going to sleep now.
まで
made
Until / to / as far as
まで marks an endpoint in space or time — "until," "as far as," or "to." Pairs naturally with から to give a range: 〜から〜まで (from … to …).
五時まで働きます。
I work until 5 o'clock.
駅まで歩きます。
I walk as far as the station.
より
yori
Than / more than
より marks the standard of comparison in comparative sentences — the thing being compared against. Equivalent to "than" in "X is bigger than Y."
東京は大阪より大きいです。
Tokyo is bigger than Osaka.
昨日より今日の方が寒いです。
Today is colder than yesterday.

Limiting Particles

限定助詞
だけ
dake
Only / just
Positive limitation
だけ limits a quantity or scope to "only" or "just." It's used with positive statements (contrast with しか which requires a negative). Relatively neutral in tone.
一つだけ食べました。
I ate just one.
あなただけが知っています。
Only you know.
しか
shika
Only (+ negative)
Always used with a negative verb — implies "nothing but"
しか always pairs with a negative verb form and means "only" with a nuance of insufficiency or exclusivity — "nothing but X," "only X (and that's all)." More emphatic than だけ.
百円しかありません。
I only have 100 yen. (implying it's not enough)
日本語しか話せません。
I can only speak Japanese.
だけ vs しか: 一つだけ食べた (I ate only one — neutral) vs 一つしか食べなかった (I only ate one — implying dissatisfaction or shortage). しか always takes a negative verb.
ほど
hodo
About / to the extent that
ほど expresses degree, extent, or approximate quantity. It also appears in the pattern 〜ば〜ほど ("the more … the more …") and in comparisons meaning "not as … as."
一時間ほど待ちました。
I waited for about an hour.
死ぬほど疲れた。
I'm so tired I could die. (extent)

Sentence-final Particles

終助詞
ね
ne
Seeking agreement
"...right?" / "...isn't it?"
ね at the end of a sentence seeks the listener's agreement or confirmation — like "right?" or "isn't it?" in English. Also softens statements and shows shared understanding.
今日は寒いですね。
It's cold today, isn't it?
おいしいですね。
It's delicious, isn't it?
よ
yo
Asserting new information
Tells the listener something they don't know
よ asserts information the speaker believes the listener doesn't know. It adds emphasis or conviction — "I'm telling you…," "you know." Combined as よね it seeks agreement while asserting.
これは本当においしいですよ。
This is really delicious, I'm telling you.
もう遅いですよ。
It's already late, you know.
